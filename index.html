Score every silent lead

Each eligible lead gets points for the signals it shows. Leads who replied, unsubscribed or bounced are left out. A higher score means a higher assumed chance that a lemon turns into a meeting.

Meeting chance per lemon is an assumed curve from 2% (score 0) to about 30% (score 100). The pilot exists to replace this assumption with real numbers.

Optimise who gets a lemon

A lemon costs more to send to the UK than to Paris, and a meeting with a 500 person company is worth more than one with a 20 person startup. Picking the top scores ignores both. The optimiser picks the set of leads with the highest expected value that fits the budget.

Send a lemon with a personal card

Every card carries a QR code with a short random code. The link holds no personal data. Scanning it opens a page made for that one lead and tells lemlist the lemon arrived and was noticed.

In a real campaign the card is printed and travels with the lemon. A lead can also claim the lemon from a link in the third email, so nobody's address is looked up without consent.

Pilot: does a lemon actually work?

lemlist reports over 20,000 B2B customers, so a new campaign step already has a channel. Before selling it, the pilot tests two questions with real campaigns.

Design

  • Who: 10 lemlist customers running B2B outbound, 100 silent leads each, 1,000 leads in total.
  • Test 1, does a lemon help? Inside each customer, leads are split at random. 500 get a lemon, 500 get the usual follow up email.
  • Outcome: meeting booked within 30 days.
  • Test 2, does the optimiser help? A later round compares lemons sent to optimised picks with lemons sent to random picks.
  • Customer side: after 30 days, ask each customer whether they would pay for it again.

What can 500 per group detect?

1,000 leads is a pilot size, not a guarantee of statistical power. If the true lift is smaller than this, the pilot will not be able to tell it apart from chance, and a bigger round is needed.
